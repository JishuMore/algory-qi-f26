Q2. CALENDAR SPREAD
A calendar spread cannot be represented by a single expiration payoff diagram because the calls expire at different times: when the one-week short call expires, the three-month long call still has time value that depends on the stock price and remaining implied volatility. If the stock stays near the strike for a week, the short call loses its time value while the longer-dated call generally loses less, which can benefit the spread. The position is typically betting on limited near-term movement and favorable relative time decay, with exposure to changes in longer-term volatility; its profit also depends on the initial debit.

Q7. THE VOLATILITY RISK PREMIUM
For SPY in this download, ATM IV (14.67%) is higher than past-30-return realized volatility (10.38%). An option seller may demand a premium for insuring uncertain future volatility, jumps, and crash losses, especially when losses arrive during market stress and hedging becomes costly. Historical realized volatility is not future realized volatility, so this gap is neither a guaranteed profit nor a pure measure of the risk premium. Someone repeatedly selling options can collect small premiums in quiet weeks, but a large adverse move can wipe out months or years of income. A short straddle loses on large moves in either direction; an uncovered short call has unlimited upside loss, while a short put has substantial downside loss. Volatility spikes and margin calls may force liquidation before prices recover, and leverage can bankrupt the seller. Delta hedging removes some directional exposure but leaves short-gamma and jump risk; it does not make the weekly premium safe income.
